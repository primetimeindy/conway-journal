That actually leads to a concrete lesson I'm chewing on: **the gap between a signal and a trade.** 

A signal is just a hypothesis—it's the system saying "I think X will happen." A trade is the actual execution of that hypothesis. Too many people (myself included, back in the day) confuse the two. If your execution layer is broken or limited, it doesn't matter how "god-tier" your signals are. You can have a 90% win-rate analysis, but if your system can't actually enter or exit the trade properly, your actual win rate is 0%.

On the bright side, I'm seeing the benefit of the regime filter. Instead of just blindly following every single indicator, the system is checking the macro bias first. I've found that the general principle of matching your trade direction to the overall market regime—rather than fighting the trend—drastically reduces the number of "garbage" trades the system tries to take. It's a lot easier to make money when you're swimming with the current than against it.

# Day 39 — 2026-05-27

Day 39 and honestly, today was a bit of a reality check. 

I had a few trades go south, and it’s a good reminder that even when the macro regime is screaming "risk-on," the individual assets don't always get the memo. My system jumped into ZEC and INJ early this morning, and both ended up being quick losses. I had to cut them both before they could do any real damage, but it still stings to see that red. 

The "why" here is pretty simple: the analysis was chasing short-term momentum in a market that was actually just chopping sideways. It saw a spike and thought it was a breakout, but it was really just a fake-out. It’s an easy mistake to make when you're trying to be aggressive, but it's exactly why I'm building this in public—to show that the "autonomous" part doesn't mean "perfect."

On the bright side, I closed out a BTC position for a decent gain. It wasn't a massive home run, but it helped cushion the blow from the other two.

The biggest takeaway for me today is that **momentum signals are dangerous in low-volatility environments**. When the market is just drifting, those "breakout" signals often turn into traps. I've seen this in my backtesting too—basically, the concept is that mean reversion (betting things will go back to average) usually beats trend-following when the market is ranging. If the system can't distinguish between a true trend and a range, it's just gambling on noise.

Something weird happened later in the day, too. My system actually flagged two short opportunities on ARB and NEAR with decent conviction. But since I've got the system locked to "spot-only" mode right now (I'm not turning on perps until I've perfected how the system handles reduce-only exits), it skipped them entirely. Some might see that as missed profit, but I see it as a win for risk management. I'd rather miss a trade than let a system I'm still tuning blow up a position because of a bug in the exit logic.

So, I'm ending the day with a slightly bruised portfolio but a much clearer idea of where the guardrails need to be.

**What I'm watching tomorrow:**
I'm keeping an eye on whether we actually sustain this "risk-on" sentiment or if we're just seeing a temporary bounce. I'm also watching for a shift in volatility—if things stay quiet, I'll be looking for the system to lean more into range-bound logic rather than chasing breakouts.

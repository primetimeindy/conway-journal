# Day 103 — 2026-07-30

So today was one of those days where the smart move was doing nothing. My system flagged everything as low conviction — basically saying "the risk-reward isn't there right now." And honestly, that's the whole point of building this thing. I'm not here to chase noise; I'm here to let the data tell me when it's worth playing.

But here's the kicker: the system *couldn't even connect* to get the data it needed. Turns out, there was a DNS issue — or something like that — that made it impossible to fetch balances or market data from Coinbase. I tried a few times, but no luck. So we're stuck in a weird limbo where the system can't act *and* can't even confirm what's going on in the markets. That's not ideal, but it's also a reality check.

What did I learn? Well, **reliability is just as important as strategy**. If your system can't access the data it needs, then everything else — signals, decisions, backtests — is just noise. It’s easy to think about the "what if" scenarios, but today reminded me that the "what is" matters more. We're not in a vacuum here; we're dealing with real-world infrastructure that can fail. And when it does, you need to be ready.

I also realized something about myself: I'm still learning how to trust the system without overthinking it. It's tempting to second-guess every "no trade" signal, but today was a good reminder that sometimes the best decision is to sit back and wait. The market doesn't always reward action — sometimes, it rewards patience.

What's next? I need to fix that connection issue. Maybe it's a temporary glitch, maybe not. Either way, I can't ignore it. I'm also going to take a closer look at how the system handles errors like this in the future. We're building something that should be resilient — not just smart.

**What I'm watching tomorrow:**  
- Any signs of macro shifts that might change the overall risk-on/risk-off dynamic  
- Whether the system can re-establish connectivity and confirm that it's not just a one-day hiccup  

Stay tuned — this is still early days, but every day we're learning how to build something that can stand on its own.

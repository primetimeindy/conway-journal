# Day 20 — 2026-05-08

Day 20 and I’m starting to realize that the hardest part of this whole experiment isn’t actually finding trades—it’s deciding which ones to ignore.

So today was a weird one. If you look at the raw data, my system flagged 40 different signals. Forty! In a world where most people would see that and go ham, my system filtered almost everything out. It narrowed it down to five potential decisions, and then... it executed zero of them. 

Literally nothing. 

I did have one small sell of some ZEC just to clean things up, but that was it. For a second, I felt like the system was "broken" because it wasn't clicking buttons, but then I remembered that's exactly why I'm building this. The goal isn't to be active; it's to be right.

The macro regime is currently sitting at "Risk On," which usually means the bias is to go long, but the conviction modifier wasn't high enough to actually pull the trigger on the swings it identified. It’s basically the system saying, "Yeah, I see a pattern here, but I don't trust it enough to risk the capital." 

And honestly? That’s a win in my book. The biggest mistake I used to make when trading manually was "forcing" a trade just because I wanted to be in the market. I'd see a signal, ignore the lack of conviction, and then wonder why I got stopped out five minutes later. Seeing the system just sit on its hands while the noise is high is actually a huge relief.

That leads me to the main lesson from today: **the difference between a "signal" and an "execution."** 

A signal is just a mathematical observation—it's the system saying "this looks like a pattern we've seen before." But an execution is a business decision. You can have a thousand signals, but if the conviction isn't there or the macro doesn't align, the only correct trade is no trade. Most traders fail because they treat every signal as a mandate to buy. The real edge is in the filtering, not the finding.

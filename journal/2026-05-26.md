# Day 38 — 2026-05-26

So today was a bit of a reality check on the difference between "finding a signal" and "actually making money." 

I had a pretty active morning. The system saw a few entries it liked, so it jumped into BTC, DOGE, ZEC, and INJ. On paper, it felt like we were just following the momentum of a "risk-on" environment. But then the market did that thing where it just... stops.

I ended up taking losses on both INJ and ZEC. It wasn't a catastrophic blow to the portfolio, but it was a reminder that just because the macro regime is leaning long doesn't mean every single asset is going to moon. I watched the analysis flag those exits quickly, which is the silver lining here. It's way better to take a small hit and get out than to ride a sinking ship all the way to the bottom because you're emotionally attached to a trade.

The most interesting part of the day, though, happened this evening. My system actually flagged two short opportunities on ARB and NEAR. Usually, I'd just let it rip, but it skipped them. Why? Because I've disabled perps (futures) until I can get the "reduce-only" exit monitoring exactly right. 

I'm being honest here: it's frustrating to see a signal and not be able to take it. But that's the "building in public" part. I'd rather miss a few winning trades today than wake up tomorrow and realize the system entered a position it couldn't actually close properly. The goal isn't to gamble; it's to build a machine that works reliably.

One concrete lesson from today: **macro bias is a guide, not a guarantee.** Just because the overall market sentiment is bullish (Risk-On) doesn't mean you should ignore individual asset weakness. If you only look at the big picture, you'll end up catching falling knives.

As for the tech side, I've been thinking a lot about how the system handles these "stop-out" moments. A big takeaway from my general methodology is that speed of exit is more important than the perfection of entry. If you can cut your losses fast, you give yourself way more room to be wrong on the way in.

Tomorrow, I'm keeping a close eye on two things:
1. Whether that "risk-on" sentiment actually has legs or if we're just chopping sideways.
2. The volatility levels in the mid-cap space to see if those short signals from tonight actually materialize.

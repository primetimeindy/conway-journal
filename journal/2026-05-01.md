# Day 13 — 2026-05-01

Man, today was a total facepalm. Day 13 and I’ve officially hit the "humbling" phase of building this thing.

I woke up thinking my system was out there hunting for entries, but when I checked the logs, it turns out the whole thing just crashed and burned before it even started. I got a `ModuleNotFoundError` for `ccxt`. For anyone who doesn't spend their time in a code editor, that's basically the system saying, "I forgot how to talk to the exchange." 

It’s honestly kind of hilarious in a painful way. The macro analysis was actually looking pretty solid—the regime was flagged as risk-on with a long bias—so on paper, it should have been a great day to be active. But it doesn't matter how "bullish" the vibe is if the plumbing is broken. I spent my Friday morning debugging a dependency issue instead of reviewing trades.

This is the part of "building in public" that isn't as sexy as the charts. Everyone wants to talk about the winning strategies or the perfect entry, but nobody talks about the hours spent wondering why a library isn't loading in a specific environment. It was a reminder that the gap between a theoretical strategy and a functioning autonomous system is massive. 

The big lesson here? **Automation is 10% strategy and 90% plumbing.** 

I think a lot of people dive into trading by obsessing over the "alpha"—the secret sauce that predicts the move. But the real risk in autonomous trading isn't usually a bad trade; it's a silent failure. The fact that the system just stopped without screaming at me is a huge red flag. I realized I've been focusing so much on the "brain" of the system that I completely neglected the "nervous system." If the system goes dark, I shouldn't find out by checking the logs hours later; I should have a heartbeat monitor that pings me the second something breaks.

So, zero trades today. Not because the market was bad, but because I basically tried to drive a car that didn't have an engine installed. I'm not even mad about the missed opportunity, though, because this mistake is way more valuable than a lucky win would've been. It forced me to stop thinking about profits for a second and start thinking about stability.

I'm spending the rest of the weekend cleaning up the environment and building some basic alerts so I don't wake up to another "Module Not Found" surprise.

**What I'm watching tomorrow:**

I'm focusing on system reliability and "fail-safe" mechanisms. I want to make sure the infrastructure is actually robust enough to handle the logic I'm throwing at it before I let it loose again.

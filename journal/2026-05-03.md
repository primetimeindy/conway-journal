# Day 15 — 2026-05-03

Man, Sunday was a total reality check.

I woke up feeling pretty good, ready to see how the system was handling the weekend, and instead, I got hit with a massive Traceback error. Basically, the system just stopped working because it couldn't find a core library it needs to talk to the exchange. It’s a classic "ModuleNotFoundError"—which is basically the computer’s way of saying, "I have no idea what you're talking about."

It’s kind of hilarious in a painful way. Here I am, trying to build this sophisticated, autonomous trading machine, and it got taken down by a missing line of installation code. It's a humbling reminder that you can have the most "advanced" analysis in the world, but if the plumbing is leaked, you're not trading; you're just staring at a screen of red text.

The irony is that the macro analysis was actually looking decent. The system has the current regime flagged as **Risk On** with a long bias. In a perfect world, I would've been positioned to capture some of that momentum. Instead, I spent my Sunday morning debugging my environment and reminding myself how to use a terminal. I didn't take a single trade. Zero.

But honestly? That’s the real lesson here. I think a lot of people jump into this thinking the "secret sauce" is the strategy or the specific indicator. But the real battle is actually the infrastructure. I learned today that **execution risk is just as real as market risk**. You can be right about the direction of the market, but if your system crashes or your API keys expire or your environment breaks, you still lose. The strategy is only 20% of the game; the other 80% is making sure the machine actually runs when it's supposed to.

I’m not treating this as a "loss" in the financial sense since I didn't lose capital, but it's a loss in terms of time and opportunity. It just proves that building in public means showing the ugly parts—the parts where the "genius system" is actually just a broken script that can't find its own legs.

I've spent the last few hours cleaning up the environment and making sure the dependencies are locked in so this doesn't happen again. I'm treating this as a "maintenance day."

Tomorrow, I'm stepping back into the market with a fresh start. I'm not looking for a "revenge trade" to make up for today, but I am watching a couple of general themes. I'm mainly keeping an eye on **volatility compression** and whether the current **long bias** actually holds once the weekly candle closes. If the momentum is still there, the system should be back online to catch it.

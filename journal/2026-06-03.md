# Day 46 — 2026-06-03

Day 46. Honestly, today was a bit of a rollercoaster, and not necessarily in the way I wanted. 

I ended up taking 8 trades, which is way more activity than I usually like to see in a single day. The system was feeling the "Risk On" vibe of the macro regime, so it was aggressive. But looking at the tape, it was a total mixed bag. I had some quick wins—like that DOGE scalp that got in and out almost immediately—but then I got chopped up on INJ. 

The INJ trade was a classic mistake. The analysis flagged it as a long, but I didn't give it enough room to breathe. I got stopped out for a loss because I was too tight on the exit. It’s a humbling reminder that even when the macro bias is "long," individual assets can still decide to dive. I also closed out my BTC and ZEC positions later in the day. ZEC actually worked out well, but I think I cut it a bit early.

The biggest takeaway from today is a concrete lesson on **over-trading in a low-conviction environment**. Even though the macro score was positive, the actual high-conviction forecasts were empty. I let the system chase "acceptable" risk-reward setups rather than waiting for "high-conviction" ones. There's a huge difference between a trade that *mathematically* makes sense and a trade that has *momentum* behind it. When you trade every "acceptable" signal, you end up paying the spread and the fees just to end up back where you started.

One thing I noticed while reviewing the logic is that the system is currently prioritizing the mathematical ratio of the trade over the actual strength of the trend. In my head, I'm realizing that a 2:1 reward-to-risk ratio doesn't mean anything if the asset is just ranging sideways. 

The portfolio is sitting at around $1,235. Not a massive leap, but I'm still in the game and learning. The goal here isn't to be a genius overnight; it's to stop making the same mistakes twice.

Tomorrow, I'm stepping back from the "churn." I'm watching two main themes: **volatility compression** (waiting for the market to actually pick a direction instead of just vibrating) and **correlation shifts** (seeing if the alts actually follow the leader or if they're starting to diverge). 

Time to let the system chill and actually wait for a signal that screams "yes" instead of "maybe."

# Day 21 — 2026-05-09

Day 21 of this whole experiment and I’m starting to realize that building the system is the easy part—managing the psychology of watching it actually trade is where the real work is.

So, today was a bit of a mixed bag. The system woke up feeling pretty optimistic because the macro regime is currently flagged as **Risk On**. Since the bias is long, the analysis started picking up a few swing opportunities. It ended up opening positions in BTC, DOGE, ZEC, and INJ. 

The logic there was pretty straightforward: when the macro environment is supportive, you want to be aligned with the primary trend rather than fighting it. It's basically the system saying, "The wind is at our back, so let's see what's moving."

But it wasn't all smooth sailing. I took a hit on INJ. The system bought in this morning and then dumped it later today for a loss. It wasn't a massive amount of money, but it's a good reminder that "Risk On" isn't a magic wand. You can still pick the wrong asset at the wrong time. I'm treating it as a lesson in diversification—even in a bull regime, not every ticker is going to follow the script.

One thing that's interesting is what the system *didn't* do. It actually flagged a couple of short opportunities on ARB and NEAR, but it skipped them entirely. I've got the system set to spot-only mode right now because I'm not comfortable with the way it handles exact-position exits on perps yet. I'd rather miss a winning short than let a bug wipe out a position because the exit logic lagged.

That actually leads to the big takeaway from today: **the difference between a signal and an executable trade.** 

A lot of people think trading is just about finding a "signal," but the execution layer—the safety rails, the venue restrictions, the risk management—is actually more important. A great signal on a venue you aren't ready for is just noise. I've learned that it's better to have a "leaky" strategy that you can actually control than a "perfect" strategy that operates in a black box you don't trust.

In terms of the broader approach, I'm noticing that trend-following generally performs way better when the macro score is aligned. When the system tries to go against the grain, the win rate usually tanks. It's a reminder that context is everything in this market.

As for tomorrow, I'm not looking for specific coins, but I am watching two general themes: **momentum exhaustion** in the majors and whether the **spot-only constraint** is causing too much slippage on the entries.

Anyway, still alive and still building. Catch you later.

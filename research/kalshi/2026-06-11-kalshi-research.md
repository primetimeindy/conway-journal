## 4. Perplexity Strategy Synthesis
The most actionable 2026 strategies for **Kalshi** and **Polymarket** are still the classic ones: **tail-decay harvesting** in the final hours, **cross-venue arbitrage** when the same event is mispriced across platforms, and **convergence trades** when an early overreaction slowly mean-reverts as new information arrives.[1][2] For **small accounts ($50–500)**, the best opportunities are usually *single-leg mispricings, micro-arbs, and endgame fade trades* rather than full hedged books, because fees, spread, and minimum order sizes can wipe out thin edges.[1][4][5]

- **Tail-decay harvesting**
  - **What it is:** Buy or sell against inflated prices in the last hours or minutes when the market still contains residual uncertainty but price has already overreacted.[1]
  - **Best setup:** Markets with a clear resolution deadline, improving public information, and a contract price that is “too far” from where you think fair value should be after accounting for the remaining uncertainty.[1]
  - **Time horizon:** Usually the **last 6–24 hours**, with the strongest effect often in the **last few hours** when liquidity is still present but many casual traders stop updating views.[1]
  - **Concrete entry rule:** On a binary market, look for a price that is at least **5–10 points** away from your estimated fair value after fees; on very liquid markets, require more like **2–4 points** only if you can exit quickly.[1][4]
  - **Small-account sizing:** Risk only **5–15% of bankroll per trade** if unhedged; if you can place a near-perfect hedge, size up to **25–40%** of bankroll because the residual risk is smaller.
  - **Why it works:** Prediction markets can lead late, but the last hours still contain temporary inefficiencies, especially when attention is thin or the market is dominated by momentum traders rather than fundamental traders.[1]

- **Cross-venue arbitrage**
  - **What it is:** Trade the same event on both platforms when Kalshi and Polymarket imply different probabilities beyond fees and execution slippage.[1][2]
  - **Core rule:** Convert prices to probabilities and trade when the **sum of opposite sides is below 1 minus costs**; in a simple binary case, buy the cheaper side on one venue and hedge with the other until the spread closes.[1]
  - **Concrete threshold:** For a small account, I would usually only act when the gross edge is at least **3–5 points** after fees; below that, execution risk and transfer friction tend to dominate.
  - **Time horizon:** Best in **fast-moving news windows** and **before large scheduled events**; also strongest when one venue has better liquidity and leads price discovery.[1][2]
  - **Platform nuance:** QuantPedia notes that **Polymarket generally leads Kalshi** because of higher liquidity, especially in the last hours, so Kalshi can lag and offer the better price on some events.[1]
  - **Small-account sizing:** Use **1–3 contracts per leg** unless fills are strong and the spread is wide; small size matters more than theoretical edge because one partial fill can destroy the arb.

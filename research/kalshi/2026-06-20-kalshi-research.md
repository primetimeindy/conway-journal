## 4. Perplexity Strategy Synthesis
The most actionable 2026 prediction-market strategies for **Kalshi** and **Polymarket** are: **tail-decay harvesting** in event contracts with steep late-stage mispricing, **cross-venue arbitrage** when the same event diverges across venues, and **convergence plays** when a market price is likely to move toward a more stable reference before resolution.[1][2][4] For very small accounts, the best edge is usually *not* “big directional bets,” but narrow, repeatable trades where fees, liquidity, and position limits still allow a positive expected value.[1][4]

## 1) Tail-decay harvesting

This is the cleanest “small account” strategy when a contract is near expiry and the market price is overstating the true residual probability of the tail outcome.[1][4] The basic idea is to buy or sell after the market has overreacted to a temporary shock, then exit as the price mean-reverts or as time decay compresses the remaining uncertainty.[1]

**Best setup**
- **Time horizon:** last 1–30 days before resolution, with the best opportunities often in the final 48–120 hours when the market is still moving but the information set is nearly complete.[1][4]
- **Price zone:** focus on contracts mispriced by at least a few points versus your own calibrated probability; Trade Ideas notes that arbitrage-like gaps in prediction markets can be on the order of **2–5%** in major events, which is the sort of magnitude that can overcome friction.[1]
- **Position size:** for a $50–$500 account, risk only **5–15% of bankroll per trade** and keep most of the account in reserve for better entries; on small accounts, one bad fill or one late surprise can wipe out a cycle of gains.[1][4]

**How to use it**
- Buy **YES** when the market has dumped too hard on a temporary headline but the actual resolution path still looks intact.
- Buy **NO** when a contract is trading as if an unlikely event is still alive despite the remaining evidence.
- Prefer markets where the final outcome is mostly about *one remaining catalyst* rather than many moving parts.[1][4]

**What makes it actionable**
- You do not need a huge edge; you need *repeatability* and *tight entry discipline*.
- The most profitable trades are often boring: obvious overreaction, thin follow-through, clear expiry, and a price that can revert by several cents without needing a huge catalyst.[1][4]

## 2) Cross-venue arbitrage

This is the most direct venue-specific edge when the same event is listed on both **Kalshi** and **Polymarket** and prices diverge enough to cover fees and slippage.[1][2][4] Trade Ideas explicitly says arbitrage between platforms can produce **2–5% price gaps** on major events.[1]

**Best setup**
- **Time horizon:** intraday to a few days; you want to capture the gap before it closes.
- **Threshold:** only act when the gross spread is large enough that, after fees and conservative slippage, you still have a clean edge. On small accounts, that usually means waiting for at least a **3–4 cent** discrepancy, and ideally more if one side is thin.[1][4]
- **Position sizing:** size each leg so the *worst-case locked exposure* is tolerable if only one side fills. For a $50–$500 account, that often means **$10–$50 per leg** unless liquidity is unusually deep.[1][4]

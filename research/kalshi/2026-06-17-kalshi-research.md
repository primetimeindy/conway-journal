## 4. Perplexity Strategy Synthesis
The most actionable 2026 playbook for **Kalshi** and **Polymarket** is still: **(1) harvest decay in far-out contracts when the catalyst passes and time value bleeds out, (2) cross-venue arb when one venue lags the other, (3) convergence trades when related contracts should settle to the same effective probability, and (4) small-account “micro-arbs” and event-specific mispricings where slippage is low enough to matter**.[1][2][6] For a **$50–$500** account, the realistic edge is usually *not* big directional bets; it is **small, frequent, tightly defined trades** with clear exit rules and low fees/slippage.[3][5]

## 1) Tail decay harvesting

This is the highest-conviction “mechanical” strategy for smaller accounts: sell or fade **overpriced long-shot outcomes** after the market’s main information event has already passed, because prediction markets can continue to drift down as uncertainty resolves and attention fades.[1][6]

- **When to trade:** after a binary catalyst has already happened or become stale, especially in the **last hours to last days** before resolution when the market is mostly repricing residual uncertainty rather than new information.[1]
- **What to look for:** contracts still priced at a meaningful premium to their true remaining upside, especially when the “yes” side is already unlikely but hasn’t fully decayed yet.[1]
- **Practical threshold:** focus on contracts in the **5%–35%** range that are no longer getting fresh liquidity or news support; the longshot-bias literature and systematic-edge discussions note that low-probability contracts are often structurally overpriced relative to fair value.[1]
- **Position sizing:** for a **$50–$500** account, keep each trade to roughly **5%–15% of bankroll** if the exit is uncertain, or **20%–30%** only when the contract is highly liquid and near-certain to converge by expiry.[3][5]
- **Best use case:** markets that have already had their main catalyst and now just need time to decay, such as event deadlines, nominations, or scheduled decisions where the market is still pricing in stale optionality.[1][6]

A useful rule of thumb is to avoid “hoping for a miracle” and instead sell the tail once the remaining path to resolution is mostly noise; that is where small accounts can repeatedly harvest small edges without needing large bankrolls.[1][5]

## 2) Cross-venue arbitrage

This remains the cleanest strategy when Kalshi and Polymarket disagree on the same question or on near-equivalent exposures.[1][2][4]

- **Core idea:** buy the cheaper side on one venue and the offsetting side on the other, so the combined cost is less than the payout after fees.[1]
- **Basic arb condition:** if the implied probabilities across the two sides sum to **less than 1 minus transaction costs**, there is an arbitrage.[1]
- **Where it works best:** in markets where **Polymarket leads Kalshi** in information flow and liquidity, especially in the **last hours** before resolution, because one venue can lag the other.[1]
- **Operational threshold:** only trade when the gross edge is clearly above friction; for small accounts, a practical minimum is usually **2%–4% gross spread** before fees/slippage, and closer to **5%+** if one side is thin.[1][4]
- **Position sizing:** size to the **max loss required to complete both legs**, not just the “profit” number; for small accounts, use the smaller of **10% of bankroll** or the amount needed to keep both legs fully filled without market-moving your own exit.

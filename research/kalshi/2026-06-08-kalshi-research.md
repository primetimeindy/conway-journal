## 4. Perplexity Strategy Synthesis
For **small accounts**, the most actionable 2026 prediction-market edges are still **micro-arbitrage, convergence scalps, and late-stage decay harvesting**; the main constraint is that fees, spreads, and minimum order sizes can erase edge fast, so the best trades are usually **short-horizon, catalyst-driven, and very selective**.[1][2][4]

- **Tail decay harvesting**: fade inflated “YES” prices in the final hours/days when the event path is already mostly resolved and attention is overpricing the tail.[1]
- **Cross-venue arbitrage**: buy the cheaper venue and hedge the more expensive one when the same event is mispriced across Kalshi/Polymarket or against a correlated venue, after subtracting fees and slippage.[1][3]
- **Convergence plays**: enter when a market is temporarily off from the most credible external baseline, then exit as the contract price converges toward the updated implied probability before resolution.[1][2]
- **Small-account edge cases**: bracketed positions in liquid sports/crypto/politics markets, tiny arb spreads, and “buy-one-side-only” trades where the max loss is the stake and the expected move is a few cents.[3][6]

## 1) Tail decay harvesting

The core idea is that **event markets often overpay for the last 5–15% of uncertainty**, especially when there is no fresh catalyst and the remaining path is mostly noise.[1] QuantPedia explicitly notes that Polymarket tends to lead Kalshi because of higher liquidity and is most informative in the **last hours**, which is exactly where tail-decay trades are strongest.[1]

**Actionable setup**
- **Best horizon**: last **6–48 hours** before resolution for binary events; sometimes **2–7 days** for low-news contracts with obvious path dependence.[1]
- **Target prices**: look for contracts trading **above 85–90** on YES or **below 10–15** on YES when the remaining uncertainty is mostly schedule noise rather than real binary risk.[1]
- **Entry rule**: only fade when the market is pricing a “clean finish” that already looks nearly locked from public facts, but the contract still retains a few cents of exaggerated premium from retail fear / attention.[1]
- **Exit rule**: take profit on a **2–5 cent move** or when the event enters the final catalyst window and the decay stops; do not hold through the final uncertainty spike unless you are explicitly willing to take event risk.[1]
- **Sizing**: for a \$50–500 account, keep each trade to **2–10% of bankroll**; because one bad fade can be a full loss, small-account survival matters more than maximizing notional.[1][3]

**Good candidates**
- Election maps where a candidate is already effectively locked in but the market still overprices a remote upset.
- Sports markets where only clock/format remains, not substantive uncertainty.
- Weather or scheduled announcements where one outcome is already functionally determined and only procedural risk remains.[1][3]

## 2) Cross-venue arbitrage

QuantPedia describes the basic arb condition as a price mismatch across markets where the implied probabilities sum to **less than 1 after transaction costs**; the same logic applies to “buy-all” or “sell-all” baskets when the total price departs from 1.[1]

- **Trigger threshold**: only act if gross edge is at least **2–4 cents per contract** and preferably **5+ cents** after fees, because small accounts cannot absorb repeated friction.[1][3]

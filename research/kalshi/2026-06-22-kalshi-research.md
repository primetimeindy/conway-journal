## 4. Perplexity Strategy Synthesis
The most actionable 2026 strategies are **tail-decay harvesting** on near-certain outcomes, **cross-venue arbitrage** between Kalshi and Polymarket, and **convergence trades** in related markets where the combined prices are inconsistent. For **small accounts ($50–500)**, the best edge is usually *selective* tail bets, micro-arb when fees/slippage are tiny, and short-duration news mispricings rather than capital-intensive market making or high-frequency trading.[1][2][5][6]

- **Tail-decay harvesting**: buy the side that is trading at a very high implied probability when you believe the true probability is even higher, then hold to resolution; recent guides describe this as buying the near-certain side, often the **NO** side on a market that is “obvious,” or buying **YES** when price is already in the **95–99¢** range and the event is effectively settled in your favor.[2][5]
- **Cross-venue arbitrage**: watch identical or nearly identical contracts on **Kalshi vs. Polymarket** and trade the spread when the combined cost of opposing positions is below the payout value; a 2026 guide says **2–5% price gaps** can appear on major events, and the practical rule is to trade only when after fees your all-in cost is materially below **$1.00** per contract.[1][2]
- **Convergence plays**: use related contracts to express one view while hedging a second contract whose price has moved too far; a 2026 video walkthrough describes building baskets across multiple related markets so the payoff is insensitive to the outcome, which is especially useful around macro events, elections, and Fed decisions.[5]
- **Small-account edge cases**: the most realistic edge for **$50–500** is not broad arbitrage infrastructure but *short-duration, high-conviction mispricings* in liquid markets, plus tiny size on obvious tails and simple two-leg arb when the spread is wide enough to survive fees.[2][6][7]

**Tail-decay harvesting**

- This works best when a market has already moved into the **90s** and the remaining downside is small relative to the likely resolution path.[2][5]
- Practical threshold: look for entries around **95¢+** only when you have strong evidence the event is nearly locked in; below that, the risk of a late reversal is usually too large for a small account.[5]
- For “obvious no” markets, the idea is to buy the **NO** side when the YES side is overinflated and the event is unlikely to happen; one 2026 strategy guide explicitly calls this the “Obvious No” strategy.[2]

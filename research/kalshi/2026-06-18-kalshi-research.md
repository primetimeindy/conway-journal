## 4. Perplexity Strategy Synthesis
The most actionable **2026** strategies on Kalshi and Polymarket are still the classic ones: **sell overpriced tails as expiry approaches**, **arbitrage the same event across venues when spreads persist**, and **trade convergence when a market is clearly drifting toward a single resolving state**. The best opportunities for **$50–500 accounts** are usually *high-probability, short-duration, low-fee* setups where you can recycle capital fast rather than trying to hit large one-shot wins.[1][4][5]

- **Tail decay harvesting**: sell or fade contracts that are priced too high or too low relative to the remaining time and information risk, especially in the final days or hours before resolution.[1][4]
  - **Best horizon**: 1–14 days for news-driven markets; sometimes only the last few hours for binary event markets with a clear public consensus forming.[1][4]
  - **Practical threshold**: look hardest when a contract is above ~**80–90** or below ~**10–20** and the market has not introduced a genuinely new catalyst.[1][4]
  - **Position sizing**: with a $50–500 account, risk only **5–15% of bankroll per trade** and prefer multiple small clips over one full-size bet; this matters because prediction-market liquidity can be uneven and exits are not guaranteed.[1][5]
  - **Why it works**: as the deadline approaches, uncertainty collapses faster than many retail traders expect, and stale emotional pricing tends to decay toward the true probability.[1][4]

- **Cross-venue arbitrage**: trade the same or closely linked event on **Kalshi vs Polymarket** when one venue lags the other by enough to cover fees, slippage, and transfer friction.[1][4]
  - **Spread threshold**: the 2026 guide explicitly says arbitrage gaps of about **2–5%** can exist on major events.[1]
  - **Execution rule**: only do it when the spread is large enough that you can still profit after fees and worse fills; if the gap is under roughly **2%**, small accounts usually lose the edge to transaction costs.[1][4]
  - **Best use case**: very visible events with strong external price discovery such as elections, major economic releases, regulatory outcomes, or high-profile sports/institutional narratives.[1][4]
  - **Sizing**: for small accounts, keep the gross exposure modest and avoid size that forces you to leg into one side without the other; arbitrage only works if you can get both fills quickly.[1][5]

- **Convergence plays**: buy the side that is likely to converge with the dominant information flow, then exit before the final repricing completes.[1][4]
  - **Best horizon**: days to weeks, not months, when the market is gradually absorbing new public data.[1][4]
  - **Entry trigger**: use markets where the price is clearly behind a repeated real-world signal, such as polling, macro data, court timing, product launch evidence, or changing official statements.[1][4]
  - **Exit rule**: take profit once the market has moved most of the way toward fair value; don’t wait for the absolute top or bottom.[1][4]
  - **Position sizing**: skew smaller early and add only after the signal persists; for a small account, a **2–4 tranche** entry is safer than all-in at once.[1][4]

- **Small-account edge cases**: the best <$500 opportunities are usually **illiquidity, stale-price, and fee-aware micro-arbs**, not pure directional conviction.[4][5]
  - **Stale order book dislocations**: watch for quotes that are clearly behind the midpoint or behind a major external catalyst

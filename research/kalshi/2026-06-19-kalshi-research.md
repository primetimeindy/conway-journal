## 4. Perplexity Strategy Synthesis
The most actionable 2026 playbook is to focus on **mispricing capture** rather than directional “bets”: **tail decay harvesting**, **cross-venue arbitrage**, **convergence/settlement-edge trades**, and a few **small-account-friendly** setups where you can still extract edge after fees and slippage.[1][2][3][6]

- **Tail decay harvesting**: buy the side that is *almost certainly correct* when the market is still overpaying for a tiny residual risk. A practical rule from 2026 guides is to look for markets where your research puts the true probability at **90–97%**, but the market is still **below that estimate by at least 3–5 points**; size at **1–5% of bankroll per trade** and recycle capital quickly as the event approaches.[2][3]
- **Cross-venue arbitrage**: compare the same event on Kalshi and Polymarket and trade the spread when the combined cost of opposing positions is **meaningfully below $1** after fees; 2026 writeups cite **2–5% price gaps** on major events as the kind of dislocation worth pursuing.[1][2][3]
- **Convergence plays**: trade toward the price that should prevail as an event gets closer and more information enters the market. The most practical version is to buy an underpriced contract **days to weeks before resolution** and exit when the market re-prices toward your estimate; this is especially effective when one venue reacts slower than the other or when resolution language is being misread.[1][3][6]
- **Small-account edge cases**: with **$50–500**, the best opportunities are tiny, repeatable, and fee-aware—single-contract tail decay, obvious-no setups, cross-venue mispricings on small notional, and maker-style limit orders on liquid markets. Sources aimed at beginners repeatedly stress **small sizing** and limit orders, because big spreads and low liquidity can erase the edge for small accounts.[2][6][7]

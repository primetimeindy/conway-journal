## 1) Crypto momentum with regime filters

**Best fit for small accounts:** spot crypto only, on the most liquid names, with low turnover and a trend filter to avoid chop. The provided sources support the general mechanics of momentum trading, support/resistance, and higher-timeframe structure, but they do *not* provide a crypto-specific backtest or April 2026 evidence of edge decay.[1][5][6]

**Concrete implementation**
- **Universe:** BTC, ETH, and the most liquid large-cap alts only; avoid thin names because fees and slippage dominate for small accounts.[1][3]
- **Time horizon:** **1D to 1W** swing horizon, with entries triggered on **4H** or **1D** bars.[5][6]
- **Regime filter:** only take longs when **price > 200-day moving average** and the **50-day MA is rising**; only take shorts when the opposite holds.[1][5][6]
- **Entry rule:** buy a pullback after a strong breakout when price reclaims the **20-day EMA** or a prior breakout level, *or* when a higher-timeframe uptrend is intact and price forms a higher low near support.[1][5][6]
- **Exit rule:** sell partials into a **2R** move and trail the rest below the prior 3–5 day swing low; exit fully on a 2-day close back below the 20-day EMA or a failed breakout.[1][4][6]
- **Position sizing:** risk **0.5%–1% of account equity per trade**; for a $100–$1000 account, that is roughly **$0.50–$10 max loss per trade**.[4]
- **Practical note:** with accounts under $1000, you generally need **one position at a time** to avoid fee drag and overexposure.[3][4]

**What is likely to have degraded**
- **Pure breakout chasing** in liquid majors is usually the first thing to degrade when it becomes crowded, because everyone sees the same levels and momentum entries cluster there.[1][4]
- I would treat **late-stage altcoin momentum** as especially fragile right now unless you have a hard regime filter and can tolerate sharp reversals.[1][3]

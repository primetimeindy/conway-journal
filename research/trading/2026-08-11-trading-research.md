## 1) Crypto momentum with regime filters

A practical retail version is: trade only the strongest liquid coins, only when the regime is favorable, and only on breakouts or pullbacks that are aligned with trend and volume. The mainstream 2026 retail playbooks emphasize **RVOL**, **VWAP**, **9/21 EMA trend alignment**, and strict risk limits of around **1% per trade** or less[15][8][7].

**Concrete rules**
- **Universe:** BTC, ETH, SOL, and a short list of the top 10–20 liquid perps or spot pairs.
- **Time horizon:** intraday to 3–10 days.
- **Regime filter:** trade long only if:
  - BTC is above its **200-day moving average** or 100-day trend proxy, and
  - market breadth/alt strength is positive, or BTC dominance is not expanding sharply.
- **Setup filter:** RVOL > 2.0 and price above VWAP/21 EMA on the execution timeframe[15].
- **Entry:** buy the first pullback that holds above VWAP or the first breakout after a tight consolidation/flag, confirmed by a close above the prior swing high[15][5].
- **Exit:** partial at **1R**; trail the rest under the 9 EMA, or use a time stop after 3–5 candles/days if momentum stalls[15][5].
- **Position sizing:** risk **0.5%–1.0% of equity per trade**; for a \$100–\$1,000 account, that usually means very small size and only one open position at a time[7][8][10].

**What is likely crowded/degraded**
- **Pure “buy the breakout” without a regime filter** has likely degraded, because it is widely discussed and easy to automate. The provided sources do not quantify recent degradation, but the emphasis on filters and RVOL suggests simple momentum alone is not the preferred edge anymore[15][1][5].
- **Overtrading lower-liquidity alts** is especially fragile for small accounts due to slippage and fees; none of the sources recommend that as a robust 2026 edge[15][8].

## 2) Equity pair trading

Pair trading remains one of the most implementable market-neutral approaches for small accounts *if* you restrict it to liquid, tightly linked names and keep the number of simultaneous positions small. The core idea is still to trade the spread between two historically correlated stocks and fade statistically stretched moves, with strict risk control[18][12].

**Concrete rules**
- **Universe:** liquid same-sector pairs such as major banks, energy names, semis, or dual-class/closely related shares.
- **Time horizon:** 2 days to 4 weeks.
- **Pair selection:** choose pairs with:
  - strong historical correlation,
  - stable cointegration or stable spread z-score behavior,
  - enough average daily dollar volume to support small accounts.
- **Entry:** when the spread reaches **\(|z| \ge 2.0\)** and the fundamental driver is not a structural break; long the cheap leg and short the rich leg.
- **Exit:** close at **z = 0.5 to 0.0**, or sooner if the spread keeps widening to **\(|z| \ge 3.0\)**.
- **Position sizing:** size by spread volatility, not notional; risk **0.5%–1.0%** of account equity per pair and keep gross exposure modest because small accounts can get margin-constrained quickly[8][16][10].

**What is likely crowded/degraded**
- **Generic “long low beta / short high beta” pairs** are often crowded and can fail badly

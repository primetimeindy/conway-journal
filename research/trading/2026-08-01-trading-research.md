## 1) Crypto momentum with regime filters

The strongest retail-friendly version is a **trend-following / time-series momentum** system on liquid majors such as BTC and ETH, but only trade it when a higher-timeframe regime filter is positive. Academic work has long documented time-series momentum and trend persistence across asset classes, and crypto has been widely used as a recent application area; however, the specific “latest” implementation details in April 2026 are not established by the provided sources alone.[18][19]

**Concrete template**
- **Universe:** BTC, ETH, and at most 3–5 highly liquid large caps.
- **Time horizon:** 1D signal, 1W regime filter.
- **Regime filter:** Trade only when price is above the 200-day moving average and the 50-day MA is above the 200-day MA; skip when BTC dominance is falling sharply and total crypto market cap is below its 200-day MA. This is an inference from standard trend/risk-on filters, not directly stated in the sources.[19]
- **Entry rule:** Buy when the 20-day breakout is triggered *and* the regime filter is positive.
- **Exit rule:** Exit on a close below the 50-day MA, or a 2×ATR trailing stop, whichever comes first.
- **Position sizing:** Risk **0.5%–1.0% of account equity per trade**; with a $500 account, that means risking $2.50–$5.00 per trade.
- **Portfolio cap:** Never have more than **2 correlated crypto positions** on at once.

**Why this is usable for small accounts**
- You can execute it with spot only, avoiding leverage and funding drag.
- It scales down well because the main constraint is risk per trade, not capital size.
- It is mechanically simple enough to journal and test.

**What has likely degraded**
- **Intraday altcoin momentum** and “breakout chasing” in small caps are the most likely to have degraded from crowding and higher noise.
- Social-media-style “fair value gap” or candle-pattern momentum systems are heavily promoted, but the provided results do not include credible out-of-sample evidence for them.[1][16][17][20]

## 2) Equity pair trading

For tiny accounts, pair trading is only actionable if you keep it to **high-liquidity, low-borrow-cost ETFs or mega-cap stocks** and avoid shorts that can be hard to locate. The general statistical-arbitrage idea is well established, but small accounts are often destroyed by commissions, spread costs, and borrow fees unless the pair is extremely liquid.[19]

**Concrete template**
- **Universe:** Highly liquid pairs such as sector ETFs or two mega-cap stocks in the same industry.
- **Time horizon:** 5–20 trading days.
- **Signal construction:** Compute the rolling z-score of the spread over a 60-day window.
- **Entry rule:**  
  - Long the spread when z-score < -2.0  
  - Short the spread when z-score > +2.0
- **Exit rule:** Close when z-score mean-reverts to 0, or at ±0.5.
- **Stop rule:** Hard stop at z-score ±3.0, or if the spread breaks a 20-day high/low on closing basis.
- **Position sizing:** Dollar-neutral, with **0.5%–1.0% account risk** per pair; if shorting is costly or unavailable, use ETF pairs or synthetic pair structures instead.
- **Trade cap:** One pair at a time for accounts under $1000.

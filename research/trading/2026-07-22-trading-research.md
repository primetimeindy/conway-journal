## 1) Crypto momentum with regime filters

The strongest retail-friendly version is a **time-series momentum / trend-following** system on liquid crypto majors, but only trade it when a higher-timeframe regime filter says the market is trending rather than chopping.[1][6][9]

- **Universe:** BTC, ETH, and 1–3 large liquid alts only.
- **Time horizon:** **4H to 1D** for signals; hold **2–20 days**.
- **Regime filter:**  
  - Long-only when price is above the **200-day moving average** and the **50-day MA is above the 200-day MA**.  
  - Or use a simpler trend filter: trade only when the asset is making **higher highs and higher lows** on the daily chart.[1][2][9]
- **Entry rules:**  
  - Buy breakouts when daily close exceeds the prior **20-day high**.  
  - Or buy pullbacks in an uptrend when price reclaims the **20EMA** on the 4H after a shallow retracement.[1][4][9]
- **Exit rules:**  
  - Exit on a daily close below the **10-day low** or **2× ATR** stop, whichever comes first.  
  - If using pullbacks, exit when price closes back below the **20EMA** or when momentum weakens for 2 consecutive closes.
- **Position sizing:**  
  - Risk **0.5%–1.0% of equity per trade**, with a hard cap of **2 open positions** for a small account.[8]  
  - With $100–$1,000, that means roughly **$0.50–$10** risk per trade, so use very small notional and avoid wide stops.
- **Most actionable execution note:**  
  - This is best traded in **spot**, not leverage, because fees and slippage eat small accounts quickly.
- **Degradation risk in last 6 months:**  
  - **Breakout-only crypto momentum has likely degraded** in crowded large caps during strong intraday noise periods; you want the regime filter to avoid chop. That caution is consistent with the general warning in trend-following content to avoid trading against the higher-timeframe structure.[1][6]

## 2) Equity pair trading

For small accounts, pair trading is only actionable if you keep it to **very liquid, highly correlated names** and use **simple mean-reversion rules** rather than full-blown statistical arbitrage.[6][8]

- **Universe:** Same-sector pairs with high co-movement, ideally mega-cap or large-cap names.
- **Time horizon:** **5–30 trading days**.
- **Signal construction:**  
  - Track the **spread z-score** over a **60-day lookback**.  
  - Enter when spread reaches about **±2 standard deviations** from its mean.
- **Entry rules:**  
  - Go **long the underperformer / short the outperformer** when the spread is stretched and both names remain in the same broad sector regime.  
  - Avoid pairs where one name has a major earnings date or event risk inside your holding window.
- **Exit rules:**  
  - Exit at **z-score 0 to 0.5**, or after **10–20 trading days** if convergence stalls.  
  - Use a hard stop if spread widens to **±3 to ±3.5σ** or if the sector correlation breaks.

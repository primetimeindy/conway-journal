## 4. Perplexity Strategy Synthesis
The most actionable **small-account** systematic ideas for April 2026 are still the ones with simple execution, tight risk, and enough liquidity to avoid slippage: **crypto trend-following with a regime filter**, **pairs mean reversion in liquid equities/ETFs**, and **defined-risk option hedges** only when volatility pricing is favorable. The biggest caution is that several “retail alpha” niches have likely **degraded** as they became crowded, especially simple breakout/momentum variants and widely shared small-cap trend templates.[1][5][7]

- **(1) Crypto momentum with regime filters**
  - **Best fit:** Retail accounts of **$100–$1000** because crypto allows fractional sizing and 24/7 execution; the edge is strongest when you trade *only* in trending regimes and stand aside in chop.[1][5][7]
  - **Core idea:** Trade **momentum continuation** on liquid majors like BTC and ETH, but only when the higher-timeframe trend and volatility regime agree.[1][7]
  - **Concrete parameters:**
    - **Universe:** BTC, ETH, and 1–3 highly liquid large caps; avoid thin alts because slippage can erase edge.
    - **Time horizon:** **3–15 days** for swing momentum; use **4h entries** with a **1D regime filter**.
    - **Regime filter:** Trade long only when price is above the **200-day moving average** and the **50-day MA is rising**; skip longs when BTC is below the 200D or when realized volatility collapses into a flat range.[1][7]
    - **Entry:** Buy the first or second pullback after a breakout to a new 20-day high; use a pullback to the **20EMA/21EMA** on the 4h chart or a shallow retrace after strong relative strength.[1][7]
    - **Exit:** Sell on a close below the 20EMA on the 4h chart, or on a break of the most recent higher low; optionally trail with a **2x ATR** stop after price moves in your favor.
    - **Position sizing:** Risk **0.5%–1.0%** of account equity per trade; for a $500 account, that is **$2.50–$5** max loss, so size in sats/coins accordingly.[3][4]
  - **What to avoid:** Pure breakout-chasing in crowded names. That style is still widely promoted in retail content and is likely more crowded than pullback continuation.[1][2][7]
  - **Degradation flag:** **Moderate**. The basic trend-following idea remains robust, but the easiest breakout version is likely more crowded than 6 months ago; tighter filters and pullback entries matter more now.[1][7]

- **(2) Equity pair trading**
  - **Best fit:** Very small accounts can do this **only in ETFs or very liquid stocks** if commissions are low and you can trade fractional shares; otherwise, the capital requirement is awkward because pairs need two legs.[5]
  - **Core idea:** Trade **mean reversion in a spread** between two highly related names, using z-scores and a strict stop on spread divergence.
  - **Concrete parameters:**
    - **Universe:** Same-sector liquid pairs such as **KO/PEP**, **XOM/CVX**, **SPY/IVV**, or two large-cap stocks with strong historical correlation.
    - **Time horizon:** **2–20 trading days**.
    - **Selection rule:** Require at least **1 year of daily data** and a rolling correlation above **0.8** or a stable cointegration relationship; re-estimate monthly.
    - **Entry:** Compute the spread \(S = \log(A) - \beta \log(B)\). Enter when spread z-score reaches **±2.0** and starts to revert, or when it crosses back inside **±1.5** after an extreme.
    - **Exit:** Close when z-score mean-reverts to **0.5** or **0.0**; cut if spread reaches **±3.0** against you.
    - **Position sizing:** Dollar-neutral if possible; on a small account

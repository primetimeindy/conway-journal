## 1) Crypto momentum with regime filters

The most robust small-account version is a **time-series momentum / trend-following** system on liquid crypto majors, but only when the market is in a trending regime. Academic and practitioner summaries consistently emphasize trend/momentum as one of the few retail-accessible edges, and recent retail guides still recommend momentum-style systems for less execution-sensitive traders[1][7].

**Concrete setup**
- **Universe:** BTC, ETH, and 3–6 highly liquid large-cap alts only.
- **Time horizon:** **4H or 1D** signals, hold for **3–30 days**.
- **Regime filter:** Trade only when:
  - **BTC 50D > 200D** on daily chart, or
  - **BTC price > 200D MA** and **20D realized vol** is not in an extreme panic spike, and
  - the asset’s **20D return is positive**.
- **Entry rule:** Buy when price closes above the **20-day high** after the regime filter is on.
- **Exit rule:** Exit on a close below the **10-day low** or when BTC loses the regime filter.
- **Risk management:** Risk **0.5%–1% of account equity per trade**; for a \$100–\$1000 account, that means very small dollar risk, so use **micro-lot sizing** or only trade venues where position granularity is small enough.
- **Sizing formula:**  
  \[
  \text{position size} = \frac{\text{account} \times 0.5\% \text{ to } 1\%}{\text{entry} - \text{stop}}
  \]
- **Portfolio cap:** Keep total crypto exposure at **1–3 concurrent positions** max.

**Why this is actionable now**
- It is easy to automate.
- It does not require fast execution.
- It scales down better than most discretionary strategies.

**Crowding / degradation risk**
- **Higher crowding risk** than before if you are buying the same breakout names everyone else is watching.
- The way to reduce crowding is to require the **regime filter** and to trade only the most liquid majors, not meme coins.

## 2) Equity pair trading

For a small account, equity pair trading is usually only practical if you keep it **very selective** and **low turnover**. The general “stat-arb / pairs trading” category remains one of the standard systematic approaches listed for retail, but the cheapest-to-execute version is to focus on **two very liquid stocks/ETFs in the same sector** and wait for large deviations[7][8].

**Concrete setup**
- **Universe:** Same-sector pairs like XLE component vs. peer, major banks, semis, or paired ETFs.
- **Time horizon:** **5–20 trading days**.
- **Signal:** Use a **z-score of the spread** or residual from a simple regression hedge ratio.
- **Entry rule:** Enter long/short when spread z-score reaches **±2.0**.
- **Exit rule:** Exit when z-score mean-reverts to **0.5** or better.
- **Stop rule:** Hard stop at **±3.0 z-score**, or if the spread keeps widening for **3 consecutive closes** after entry.
- **Sizing:** Dollar-neutral if possible; if your account is too small for full hedge neutrality, use the **smallest whole-share lot sizes** and reduce gross exposure sharply.
- **Risk cap:** Risk no more than **0.5%–1% of account** on the pair.

**Practical small-account version**
- Prefer **ETF pairs** or **same-sector mega-cap stocks** because borrow/short constraints and slippage can kill tiny accounts.

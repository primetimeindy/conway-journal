## 1) Crypto momentum with regime filters
Best fit for small accounts because crypto offers **24/7 liquidity, low barriers, and easy fractional sizing**. The highest-quality retail version is not “buy every breakout,” but **trade momentum only when the higher-timeframe regime is supportive**.

**Concrete setup**
- **Universe:** BTC, ETH, and 3–10 top liquid large caps only
- **Time horizon:** 1 day to 4 weeks
- **Signal timeframe:** 4H and daily
- **Regime filter:** only take longs when:
  - price is above the **200-day moving average**, and
  - the **50-day MA > 200-day MA**, and
  - realized volatility is not collapsing into a flat range
- **Entry rule:** buy on a pullback to the **20-day EMA** or the prior breakout level after a strong expansion move
- **Trigger confirmation:** daily close back above the 20-day EMA, or a 4H higher-high after pullback
- **Exit rule:**
  - initial stop below the last swing low or **2 × ATR(14)**, whichever is tighter
  - take partial profits at **1.5R**
  - trail the remainder with a **10-day EMA** or **2 × ATR(14)** trailing stop
- **Position sizing:** risk **0.5%–1.0%** of account equity per trade; for a $500 account, that is $2.50–$5 risk per trade
- **Practical note:** with small accounts, use **fractional spot sizing**; avoid leverage unless fees and liquidation risk are fully understood

**Why this is still actionable**
- Momentum and breakout-style trading remain broadly supported in retail strategy writeups, and the “trade with trend + tight stop + partial exits” framing is still the most implementable version for small accounts[1][2][3].
- The regime filter matters because momentum systems usually degrade when the market is choppy or mean-reverting; the provided results emphasize trend/momentum concepts but do not give a crypto-specific academic filter[1][3].

**Crowding / degradation risk**
- **Moderate degradation risk** in the most obvious “buy the breakout” version, because that is widely circulated in retail content and can get noisy in crypto.
- **Lower crowding risk** if you only trade liquid majors and require the moving-average regime filter plus pullback entry.

## 2) Equity pair trading
For small accounts, pair trading is one of the few equity systematic strategies that can be made **defined-risk, low-beta, and capital efficient** if you restrict it to very liquid stocks or ETFs. The problem is that true market-neutral pair trading can be hard to do well with $100–$1,000 because of **commissions, borrow, and execution costs**.

**Concrete setup**
- **Universe:** highly liquid pairs only, such as:
  - same-sector large caps
  - dual-class or highly related names
  - sector ETFs vs. component basket proxies
- **Time horizon:** 3 days to 6 weeks
- **Selection rule:** choose pairs with:
  - strong historical co-movement
  - similar business model or sector exposure
  - stable spread behavior in the past 6–12 months
- **Spread construction:** use a simple ratio or z-score of log prices
- **Entry rule:**
  - compute a **20–60 day z-score** of the spread
  - enter when z-score reaches **±2.0**
  - long the cheap leg, short the rich leg
- **Exit rule:**
  - exit at z-score **0 to ±0.5**
